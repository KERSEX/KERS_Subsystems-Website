Your race,
in numbers.

KERS Subsystems listens to the telemetry stream from F1 26 and turns it into an overlay that makes the situation readable at a glance: timing tower, track map, battles, pit stops, penalties. As a frameless window on top of the game — and as its own window for recording in OBS.

On top of the game

A frameless window, always on top. Locked, every click passes through — you keep playing normally while the overlay sits above it.

In OBS

A second, opaque window at canvas size. Pick it up in OBS with a window capture, colour key on magenta — done.

From your phone

Control and settings are the two pages in the browser. On the same network you steer the overlay from your phone — cleared once with the code from the control panel, right down to the camera in the game.

New in version 0.3.1

Now with an F1 TV look.

A second design recreates the TV graphics. On top of that you control OBS straight from the control page, count the championship with your own points — and other devices need a code.

Recreation of the “F1 TV” design — in use the values come from the game.

Two designs

KERS or F1 TV: full names, tyres as a round disc with their age, gap and interval taking turns, target time like on TV. Or your own design from a copy.

OBS from the control page

Scenes, sources, audio, transitions, stream and recording — via OBS's WebSocket server, straight from the control page. From your phone too.

Team standings, own points

Drivers, teams or both in turn. You enter the points system P1–P20 in the settings, and it travels with the championship file.

Access with a code

Only this PC gets in as it is. Every other device enters the 6-digit code from the control panel once; devices and IPs can be blocked.

Twitch chat, direct

KERS now reads the chat itself — which also works in browsers that block the Twitch embed.

Hot laps stay put

After the line the time stays for 5 s, even if the next flying lap follows straight away. And no more made-up purple S3.
